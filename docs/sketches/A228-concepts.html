Atlas skill map: three ways to show it

Each concept answers the same five questions from the same counted data. They differ in what leads. One synthetic person (a mid-level backend engineer aiming at Data Engineer) is shown in each, on a seeded board. No model is called.

  1. My skill map: what I have, and how strongly it's shown (proven, on my résumé, not yet).
  2. My gaps: what the role asks that I lack, ranked by how many postings ask for it.
  3. Where I stand: how many open postings I already meet, against all of them, by level. No surface in draft answers this today.
  4. How to close it: the next skill, the order, the proof for each, and the postings each one opens.
  5. A pivot: the roles next to mine, what carries over, the market size, and the cost to move.
Concept A

One map

The skill map leads: a treemap of your skills by family, each square sized by how many postings ask for it and filled by how far you've shown it. A role switch resizes the same squares, so a pivot is the same picture. Where you stand and the route sit beside it.

Strongest on Q1 and Q5. Q3 and Q4 are side panels.

 Concept B

The readout

One number leads: postings within reach today, split by level. Below it, one chart per question: the distribution of postings by how much of each you meet, ranked gap bars, a step chart of the route, and a scatter of adjacent roles by overlap and market.

Strongest on Q2 and Q3. The most direct to build from draft's counts.

 Concept C

The route

The journey leads: a line of stations from you today to the aim, each column showing postings within reach once that skill is proven. Other roles follow as small multiples on one scale. The skill map becomes a skills × roles grid.

Strongest on Q4 and Q5. Closest to the track board Atlas has now.
